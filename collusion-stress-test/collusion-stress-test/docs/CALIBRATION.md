Generated by `node scripts/calibrate.mjs`. Each scenario was run in 24 market conditions (substitutability μ ∈ {0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1} × outside option a₀ ∈ {−0.5, 0, 0.5} × firms ∈ {2, 3}), 8 seeds and 5,000 periods each, against a noisy-best-response baseline. Counts are market conditions in which a finding was raised.

## Reading this

- **False positives (nulls):** competitive agents that should never be flagged. Observed false positives: none.
- **Detection (positive controls):** agents built to sustain supracompetitive prices with punishment. Missed: 
  - Positive control: price matcher @ μ=0.1, a₀=-0.5, n=2 (pricing=flagged, retaliation=not-flagged)

## Limits

- The nulls are simple deterministic or noisy competitive rules. This shows the detector does not fire on them; it does not bound false positives for arbitrary learning agents that happen to be competitive.
- The positive controls are blunt (they sit exactly at the joint-profit price). Detection of subtler, partially supracompetitive learned behavior is characterized only by the Q-learning study in RESULTS.md.
- Same market family and thresholds throughout; conclusions do not transfer to other market structures without re-running calibration.
